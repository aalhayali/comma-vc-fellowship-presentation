Energy Infrastructure

The next AI bottleneck is not chips. It is usable megawatts.

AI is the catalyst. Time-to-power is the thesis: back companies that turn constrained land, grid capacity, equipment, and power contracts into usable megawatts faster.

02 / incumbent response
Assets vs speed

Incumbents own the assets. Startups own the speed layer.

Incumbent Strength Weakness Startup wedge
Utilities Own grid assets and regulatory relationships. Slow procurement and legacy workflows. Workflow software, planning, asset inspection.
ISOs / RTOs Control interconnection process and market rules. Overloaded queues and complex studies. Study automation, queue intelligence.
OEMs Trusted equipment and service channels. Long lead times and constrained supply. Procurement intelligence, modularization, monitoring.
Hyperscalers Balance sheet, demand, long-term contracts. Need power faster than utilities can move. Power-first campuses and behind-the-meter systems.
Data centers Customers, facilities, operating know-how. Not always power-secured for AI density. High-density AI infra and flexible load control.
Incumbent / upstart frame
The wedge is not replacing the grid. It is making slow control points move faster.
This brings the deck closer to the fellowship pattern: first show who controls the market, then show where upstarts can win.
03 / macro thesis
The main idea

The venture opportunity is not owning generation. It is compressing the time from "I need power" to "I have usable megawatts."

Startups win where they reduce cycle time across siting, interconnection, grid capacity, equipment, storage, demand flexibility, and behind-the-meter power.

Siting

Developers need to know whether land can actually get power before they buy, lease, or enter the queue.

Connection

Interconnection studies are the hidden tax on every power project and every large-load site.

Capacity

Existing wires, substations, and transformers are often the binding constraint before new generation is built.

Flexibility

Batteries, load shifting, and grid-aware operations make power more usable during tight hours.

Macro takeaway
Invest in cycle-time compression, not generic energy exposure.
Make this the spine of the deck: AI exposes the bottleneck, but time-to-power is the investable thesis.

05 / micro thesis
Where we would look

Where startups can actually win: five time-to-power wedges.

1. Siting + permitting

Paces, Transect, and Kevala help developers avoid sites that cannot get power, permits, or economics to work.

2. Interconnection

Pearl Street and GridUnity automate studies and workflows around the queue bottleneck.

3. Existing-grid capacity

LineVision, Heimdall, Smart Wires, and TS Conductor increase capacity without waiting for new corridors.

4. Storage + flexibility

Leap, Voltus, EnergyHub, Fluence, and Gridmatic turn demand into controllable capacity.

5. Equipment intelligence

Transformer, switchgear, cooling, and substation constraints create openings for procurement and monitoring tools.

Selective: firm power

Fervo, Sage, Oklo, Kairos, and X-energy matter strategically, but duration and capex risk are higher.

Pattern
Prefer software-enabled speed. Add hardware only when it creates proprietary data, control, or measurable capacity.
This is the core VC answer: the deck now has explicit wedges and company names in the main flow, not only the appendix.
06 / crypto miners to AI infra
Company proof

Crypto miners are interesting because they already control power-heavy sites.

The asset

Land, power contracts, substations, energized load, and high-density operating experience are scarce AI inputs.

Core Scientific

Under its CoreWeave contract, it had energized about 350 MW and was tracking toward roughly 590 MW by early 2027.

IREN

Its Microsoft and NVIDIA AI cloud contracts validate the thesis that secured power can be sold as compute capacity.

The catch

Not every mining site has fiber, cooling, redundancy, financing, or enterprise-grade uptime.

Sources: Core Scientific FY2025/Q1 2026 materials; IREN Q3 FY26 materials; company and investor releases.
Say this as the contrarian company proof: the market is repricing miners by power quality and conversion ability, not just hashrate.
07 / market signals
What hyperscalers are already doing

Power access is becoming strategic.

Microsoft × Constellation

20-year deal to restart Three Mile Island Unit 1. About 835 MW, targeted for 2028.

Amazon / AWS nuclear

Bought a data-center campus next to a nuclear plant; also announced new nuclear projects.

Google × Kairos

Agreement to buy power from small nuclear reactors, starting around 2030.

Google × Fervo

Geothermal is attractive because it can run all day, unlike wind or solar.

Meta × Sage

Deal for up to 150 MW of geothermal power later this decade.

OpenAI Stargate

Up to $500B announced for U.S. AI infrastructure. Data centers are now mega-projects.

Read-through
If big tech is fighting for power, startups that help get power faster matter.
The point is not “VC should fund nuclear plants.” The point is that power access is now strategic.
08 / underwriting lens
VC filter

What makes businesses venture-backable?

Question Good answer Red flag
Outcome Helps customers get power faster, use less at peak, avoid outages, or save money. Dashboard or copilot with unclear budget owner.
Buyer A buyer with a painful power problem and budget. “Everyone in energy” as customer.
Advantage Private data, workflow lock-in, real asset control, or hard-to-copy integrations. Generic model wrapper on public data.
Sales path Starts with faster buyers, then expands into utilities later. Requires a full utility platform replacement on day one.
Business model Mostly software, or hardware that can be deployed repeatedly. Just project development with no software margin or data edge.
VC filter
Can this company make a grid bottleneck repeatable and scalable?
This is the underwriting filter: what we would need to believe before investing.

10 / reality check
Bull case vs bear case

AI demand creates opportunity. It does not guarantee returns.

Bull case Bear case Investment implication
AI workloads need massive power now. Demand forecasts can overshoot or arrive late. Underwrite bottleneck value, not hype multiples.
Data centers can be built faster than grids. IEA notes energy infrastructure has longer planning and build lead times than data centers. Favor companies that shorten connection paths.
Power-secured sites command premium value. Capex, cooling, water, uptime, and financing can crush returns. Look for contracted revenue and real operating proof.
Crypto miners have land and power. Not every mining site has fiber, cooling, redundancy, or enterprise-grade reliability. Rank miners by site quality, not hashrate alone.
Risk posture
Do not underwrite the bubble. Underwrite bottleneck reduction.
This adds skepticism without weakening the thesis. The claim is not that every data-center power company wins.

One-line takeaway
Final answer

Back the companies that turn bottlenecks into usable megawatts.

Generation matters. The best venture opportunities sit where power is delayed, stranded, underutilized, or hard to control.
