The next AI bottleneck is not chips. It is usable megawatts.

AI needs power, the grid is slow, and startups can help close the gap.

02 / physical bottlenecks
Why projects slow down

The grid is constrained in multiple places.

Transformers

Large transformers can take years to procure, delaying substations, factories, and data centers.

Transmission lines

Building new transmission is slow because of permitting, land use, and regional coordination.

Interconnection studies

Utilities are overwhelmed by the number of projects requesting grid access.

Substations

Many regions do not have enough nearby infrastructure to add large new electricity demand quickly.

Utility labor

Utilities face shortages of engineers, operators, and field workers, slowing planning and approvals.

Firm power

Data centers need electricity around the clock, not only when solar or wind output is high.

Why this matters
Software matters because physical infrastructure takes years to expand.
This slide explains why the bottleneck exists in practice. The issue is not a single shortage. The entire system is under pressure at once.
03 / macro thesis
The main idea

AI data centers need power faster than the grid can provide.

That creates the opening for startups: help companies find power, connect to the grid, and use existing grid capacity better.

Demand is moving fast

AI data centers can appear in a region faster than utilities can build new lines, substations, and power plants.

The grid moves slowly

Getting connected can take years because of studies, permits, transformers, and transmission constraints.

Always-on power matters

Data centers need electricity all day, not just when solar or wind is producing.

Flexibility helps

Batteries, buildings, EV chargers, and factories can reduce demand during tight hours and make the grid easier to manage.

Macro takeaway
The opportunity is not just making more power. It is getting usable power to the right places faster.
Avoid the jargon here. The macro thesis is simple: AI demand is fast, the grid is slow, and startups can help close that timing gap.

05 / micro thesis
Where we would look

Micro thesis: back startups that remove grid bottlenecks.

1. Pick better sites

Before building, developers need to know whether a location can actually get power. Bad sites waste years.

2. Connect sooner

Some projects can connect earlier if they agree to use less power when the grid is stressed.

3. Use existing wires better

Sensors and software can show when power lines have extra room, instead of assuming they are always full.

4. Make demand flexible

Batteries, EV chargers, thermostats, and buildings can act like a small power plant by reducing demand at the right times.

5. Fix equipment bottlenecks

Transformers and substation equipment are hard to get. Startups can help source, monitor, and reuse them.

6. Help data centers buy power

AI data centers need help finding power, signing power deals, and managing backup power and storage.

Pattern
Prefer software. Add hardware only when it gives useful data or control.
This is the core VC answer. The point is not “build more solar.” The point is: help projects get through the grid faster.
06 / AI in the space
Demand + tools

AI is increasing electricity demand. It can also help energy companies work faster.

AI creates new demand

Data centers used ~460 TWh globally in 2022 and could pass 1,000 TWh by 2026.

AI can speed up slow work

Utilities and developers spend months on studies, permits, forecasts, inspections, and outage planning.

AI is useful when it changes action

The best tools do not just summarize. They help operate batteries, buildings, EV charging, or backup power.

The moat is real-world data

The defensible companies will have private grid data, hard integrations, audit trails, and control over assets.

Sources: IEA Electricity 2024; Goldman Sachs AI power demand, 2024; DOE/LBNL data center report.
Say this cleanly: generic chatbots are not the thesis. The thesis is AI that helps energy teams make faster, safer operating decisions.

What hyperscalers are already doing

Power access is becoming strategic.

Microsoft × Constellation

20-year deal to restart Three Mile Island Unit 1. About 835 MW, targeted for 2028.

Amazon / AWS nuclear

Bought a data-center campus next to a nuclear plant; also announced new nuclear projects.

Google × Kairos

Agreement to buy power from small nuclear reactors, starting around 2030.

Google × Fervo

Meta × Sage

Deal for up to 150 MW of geothermal power later this decade.

Read-through
If big tech is fighting for power, startups that help get power faster matter.
The point is not “VC should fund nuclear plants.” The point is that power access is now strategic.
08 / underwriting lens
VC filter

What makes businesses venture-backable?

Question Good answer Red flag
Outcome Helps customers get power faster, use less at peak, avoid outages, or save money. Dashboard or copilot with unclear budget owner.
Buyer A buyer with a painful power problem and budget. “Everyone in energy” as customer.
Advantage Private data, workflow lock-in, real asset control, or hard-to-copy integrations. Generic model wrapper on public data.

09 / highest-conviction wedges
If we had to pick 3

Three categories where startups can make an impact.

Find better grid sites

Why: developers can lose years pursuing projects that cannot realistically get power.

The best companies help customers understand grid availability before they buy land, sign leases, or enter the queue.

  • Start: site screening + grid data
  • Later: permits, financing, power contracts

Power for data centers

Why: AI infrastructure is becoming constrained by power access, not just chips or servers.

Data centers increasingly need help securing reliable power, managing flexible demand, and operating their own energy infrastructure.

  • Start: find power + manage flexible load
  • Later: storage, backup power, grid services

Use the existing grid better

Why: building new transmission takes years, but many existing lines are not used efficiently today.

Sensors and software can help utilities safely move more electricity through the grid they already have.

  • Start: sensors + line monitoring
  • Later: utility planning + congestion management
Micro conclusion
The best startups reduce the time, cost, or uncertainty required to get usable power.
Keep this slide practical and grounded. The point is not advanced energy technology. The point is helping customers get power faster, more reliably, and with less uncertainty.
10 / execution risks
Risks

What could slow adoption or weaken returns.

Utility sales cycles

Utility sales can take 12–24 months. Start with faster buyers when possible.

Regulatory friction

Regulation can slow everything down. The product needs clear audit trails.

Hardware liability

Hardware touches safety, warranty, and cyber risk. Reliability has to be real.

Demand overbuild risk

Some data-center forecasts will be wrong. The startup should still work if AI demand cools.

Risk posture
Do not make the thesis depend on AI hype.
This slide adds skepticism. It makes the deck more credible.
